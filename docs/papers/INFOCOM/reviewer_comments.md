| The main contribution of this paper is to apply LLM to RAN scenarios. Although this idea is timely, this paper lacks in the innovativeness of the method and the richness of experimental simulations. | This paper studies the scenario of intent-based networking (IBN) for Radio Access Network (RAN) management, where the authors propose to leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for intent translation and closed-loop optimization. However, the paper lacks a detailed and rigorous mathematical description of the considered system model. The proposed method appears to mainly apply existing LLM techniques without introducing substantial innovation, and the experimental section does not provide meaningful comparisons with existing approaches. Below are the detailed comments:<br><br>1. System modeling: Although the paper focuses on the RAN scenario, it does not provide a rigorous formulation of the optimization or intent translation problem. <br><br>2.Novelty: While the integration of LLMs with IBN is discussed, the paper mainly demonstrates structured prompting and JSON-based interfaces. These contributions appear incremental rather than fundamentally novel.<br><br>3.Missing comparisons: The paper does not compare the proposed approach against existing ML-based or optimization-based RAN management schemes. Without such comparisons, it remains unclear whether LLM integration brings significant advantages over established methods.     | The paper should include a rigorous mathematical formulation of the system model, provide fair comparisons with existing ML- or optimization-based RAN management schemes, and expand experiments to larger-scale scenarios. Clarifying the novelty beyond structured prompting would also strengthen the overall contribution and technical impact.    |
